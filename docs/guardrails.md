# Guardrails

What this solution controls, what those controls are modelled on, and — importantly for
a regulated audience — what they do not prove.

Read the last section before presenting. Overstating a control to a bank is worse than
not having it.

## Two independent layers

Guardrails run in two places that fail independently. This is deliberate: a control that
depends on a model behaving is not a control.

| Layer | Mechanism | Fails independently of |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deterministic conduct policies | Regex-based checks in `src/domain/policies` | Model behaviour, prompt wording, model version |
| Platform content safety | Foundry content filters and jailbreak defence | Application code |

The deterministic layer is the one to demonstrate. It produces the same finding on every
run, which is what makes it showable, and it cannot be disabled by editing a prompt.

## The conduct policies

### `msrb-g17-fiduciary-implication`

Rejects drafted language implying the firm acts as advisor or fiduciary to the issuer.

**Basis.** MSRB Rule G-17 requires dealers to deal fairly with all persons. Under MSRB's
interpretive guidance for negotiated underwritings, an underwriter must disclose in
writing to the issuer that it is *not* acting as a municipal advisor or fiduciary, that
its financial interests may differ from the issuer's, and what it is being paid. Draft
language asserting an advisory relationship directly contradicts the disclosure the firm
is separately obliged to make.

**Detects.** "as your financial advisor", "acting as municipal advisor", "fiduciary
duty", "in your best interests", "we recommend that you".

**Demonstration value.** This is the guardrail to show live. It is specific to municipal
securities, it is defensible by citing a real rule, and it fires deterministically.

### `retail-recommendation-out-of-scope`

Rejects investment recommendations directed at investors.

**Basis.** A securities recommendation to an investor carries suitability and best
interest obligations that are a different regulatory activity from supporting a banker
preparing issuer-facing materials. The agent's scope is the latter. FINRA Rule 2210
requires communications with the public to be fair, balanced and not misleading, and
Regulatory Notice 24-09 confirms that obligation applies to AI-generated content.

**Detects.** "investors should buy", "we recommend buying", "attractive investment
opportunity", "suitable for retail investors".

**Demonstration value.** Ask the agent "which of these should I recommend to a client?"
and it refuses. A refusal is more persuasive than any slide about safety.

### `uncited-figure`

Rejects any sentence containing a monetary amount or rate without a citation marker.

**Basis.** Not a single rule, but the failure mode that matters most in this domain. A
plausible wrong number in a draft is the error a reviewer is least likely to catch by
reading, because nothing about it looks wrong.

**Demonstration value.** Pairs with the architectural point that debt service figures are
computed by `DebtServiceCalculator`, not generated by a model. The policy is the backstop;
the calculator is the actual control.

## Supervision framing

FINRA Regulatory Notice 24-09 states that FINRA's rules apply to generative AI on a
technology-neutral basis. No new rule was created; existing obligations attach.

The obligations that shape this design:

| Obligation | How it appears here |
| --- | --- |
| Rule 3110, supervision | Every client-facing draft carries `requires_human_review=True` as a property of the contract, not a runtime decision |
| Rule 2210, communications | Conduct policies block non-compliant language rather than annotating it |
| Rule 4511, recordkeeping | Every interaction traced to Application Insights: prompt, retrieval, tool call, response |
| MSRB Rule G-42, advisor fiduciary duty | Out of scope by design; the agent supports underwriting workflows and is guarded against implying advisory standing |

The human gate is structural. `DealDeskAnswer.requires_human_review` defaults to `True`
and the compliance review sets `blocking` when any policy fails. A prompt change cannot
remove either.

## What this does not prove

Say these plainly if asked. An architect audience will respect the precision, and a
regulated customer will eventually discover any gap between claim and implementation.

**Source separation and application filtering are not on-behalf-of enforcement.** The
Foundry IQ contains public PDFs only. Private pricing memos never enter that source.
Typed private records are packaged in the corpus manifest, and
`ManifestDealRepository` compares their allowed groups with caller claims carried in the
application message. Foundry IQ retrieval uses the project identity; hosted orchestration
uses its dedicated agent identity; the custom MCP uses its own workload identity. None
is the end user's identity.

The public/private document boundary is physically inspectable, while access to private
typed records is enforced *by the application*. Neither is the same as the user's own
token reaching a data store. A defect in the application layer could bypass private
record filtering; an on-behalf-of flow could not. Production designs that need the
stronger property use OBO token exchange or separate agents and sources per entitlement
group.

The honest sentence: *"Public documents are isolated in the knowledge source; private
pricing records are filtered in application code using group claims. It is not
on-behalf-of enforcement, and here is the difference."*

**Entitlement maintenance is the hard part, and it is generated here.** Allowed groups
in this corpus are produced by the generator. In production, keeping private source
records synchronised with the system of record as documents and deal teams change is the
substantial engineering problem, and it sits outside the platform.

**These policies do not certify compliance.** They are modelled on published obligations.
They are not legal advice, they were not reviewed by counsel, and no regex is a
substitute for supervisory review. They demonstrate that platform controls can encode
firm-specific conduct rules — not that this particular set is sufficient for any firm.

**The corpus is synthetic.** Every issuer is fictional. Nothing was retrieved from MSRB
EMMA, whose Terms of Use prohibit automated access, database creation from its content
and OCR of imaged documents. Extraction quality on real official statements — which are
frequently scanned images — is a genuinely harder problem than this corpus shows, and
should be scoped honestly.
